Wingbeat Pipeline

What the instrument does, from a mosquito in a cup to a number a health office can act on — and which parts of that path are finished.

Built and verified

The whole measurement path. Checked numerically against the Python original at every stage, and in a real browser, offline.

Measured, with a caveat

The detector is right 98% of the time when it says mosquito. The Culex head is right 55% of the time, so it screens rather than identifies.

A

Training the models

done, worth retraining Once, on Google Colab with a GPU. Never on the phone.

  1. 1  Take the only public dataset there is

    HumBugDB, from Zenodo. It is the largest public collection of mosquito flight audio, and it decides what can and cannot be learned here.

    9,295 files · 33.5 hours
    Tanzania, Thailand, United Kingdom, Kenya, USA
    Indian recordings: none

  2. 2  Cut it into one-second examples

    A log-mel spectrogram is the standard representation for this task. The mel bands are capped at 2 kHz, which puts the resolution where a wingbeat fundamental and its first two harmonics actually live.

    1 s windows, 0.5 s hop · silent windows dropped
    40 mel bands, 100–2000 Hz · n_fft 512, hop 128
    one example = 40 × 63

  3. 3  Split by recording, never by window

    Two windows from one recording are nearly the same sound. Split them across train and test and the model scores beautifully while having learned nothing. This is the single most common way an acoustic result turns out to be false.

    GroupShuffleSplit on recording id · 25% held out
    asserted: no recording appears in both halves

  4. 4  Train two small networks

    Small on purpose. It has to run on a mid-range phone with no network, so the whole model is smaller than a photograph.

    Model 1

    Is a mosquito flying near this microphone?

    1. Trained on 18.3 h of mosquito against 14.6 h of background
    2. Species independent, so it travels between places better than any species model could

    Useful on its own. A trap that counts mosquito-minutes is a surveillance instrument before it can name anything.

    Model 2

    Is that mosquito Culex quinquefasciatus?

    1. Trained on 678 files, 124.6 min — the most any single species in the dataset offers
    2. A real Tamil Nadu vector: lymphatic filariasis, Japanese encephalitis

    A hypothesis, not a result. Wingbeat frequency follows body size, and body size differs between populations.

    Conv16 → pool → BN → Conv32 → pool → BN → Conv64 → GAP → Dense1 sigmoid
    23,553 parameters · 92 KB each · class weights, early stopping

  5. 5  Carry the model to the phone

    Weights, the mel filterbank and the training normalisation all travel together. The normalisation matters: feed the model numbers centred differently from the ones it learned on and it does not fail, it answers confidently and wrongly.

    detector mean −27.447 sd 10.833
    culex    mean −27.827 sd  9.843
    filterbank exported from librosa, not recomputed

  6. 6  Prove the JavaScript computes the same thing

    The phone runs a reimplementation in plain JavaScript with no machine-learning library. Every stage is checked against the Python original, because a small mismatch here produces confident nonsense rather than an error.

    Two numbers matter and they disagree. Read only the ROC AUC and both models look excellent. Read the precision and the Culex head turns out to be a screening filter rather than an identifier.

    What these two columns actually mean

    The detector is deployable: it finds 92 of every 100 mosquito windows and is right 98 times in 100 when it says mosquito. The Culex head catches nearly every Culex but almost half of what it calls Culex is something else, so it is a filter for deciding which recordings deserve a microscope, not a statement about what a mosquito was. A ROC AUC of 0.98 beside a precision of 0.55 says the ranking is good and the threshold is wrong.

  8. 8  Retrain with an honest validation split — not done

    Both models were rolled back by early stopping to almost the start of training, because the signal it was watching was meaningless. Everything above is a floor, not a ceiling.

    The bug, and why the scores survived it

    Keras validation_split takes the last 15% of the array without shuffling, and the arrays are built in recording and species order. So the validation block was one unrepresentative clump: the Culex run reported validation accuracy between 0.20 and 0.46 against a 0.12 positive rate, which is worse than answering “no” every time. Early stopping restored epoch 1 for the Culex head and epoch 2 for the detector. The held-out test split was done properly by recording, so the numbers above are real — they are just the scores of two under-trained models.

    fix: carve the validation set out of the training groups with a second GroupShuffleSplit
    also save the held-out prediction array, so a threshold can be chosen rather than assumed

B

Measuring one mosquito

built and verified Every recording. On the phone, with no network, 227 KB of models cached.

  1. 1  Record eight seconds

    The distance is not a preference, it is the limit of the method. Published work found a phone reaches a mosquito at about 50 mm for a basic handset and 100 mm for a better one. Beyond that there is no signal, however good the software.

    5–10 cm · below about 50 dB background
    echo cancellation, noise suppression and automatic gain all switched off
    all three are designed to remove exactly this kind of faint steady tone

  2. 2  Two independent paths read the same audio

    One is arithmetic and always available. The other is learned and carries a caveat about where its training data came from. They are kept apart on screen because they answer different questions, and where they disagree, that disagreement is itself information.

    Path 1 · arithmetic

    What frequency is it beating at?

    1. 20 ms windows, 10 ms hop, Hann window
    2. FFT, then harmonic product spectrum — summed logs with a floor, so one missing harmonic cannot wipe out the score
    3. Parabolic interpolation on the peak, for sub-bin precision
    4. Median, 5th–95th percentile, histogram

    A frequency in Hz, and a band. Recovers a known 450 Hz tone to about 1 Hz, including when the fundamental is quieter than its own harmonics.

    Path 2 · learned

    Is it a mosquito, and is it Culex?

    1. Windowed-sinc resample to 8 kHz (a box average folded 6 kHz noise back into the band and was replaced)
    2. 1 s windows, 0.5 s hop; silent windows skipped, not scored
    3. Log-mel 40 × 63, normalised with the training mean and sd
    4. Detector on every window; Culex only on windows the detector accepted

    Mosquito-seconds and a per-window confidence strip. Asking “is this Culex” about empty room noise gets a confident answer to a question with no answer.

    Why mosquito-seconds and not an average

    A mosquito does not fly past a microphone for eight seconds. It passes. A mean across windows would bury one clear pass in fourteen empty ones. Overlapping detected intervals are merged and their union measured, so summed over a night this becomes mosquito-minutes — the standard trap statistic.

  3. 3  Label it from the microscope

    This is the step that makes everything else worth keeping. Record first, then kill and identify under a microscope, then type it in. A recording without a confirmed identification trains nothing and proves nothing.

    species · sex · site · temperature °C · method · GPS · notes
    temperature matters: wingbeat frequency moves with it

  4. 4  Store it, and get it off the phone

    The measurements are useful. The raw audio is irreplaceable. Both leave the device, along with whatever the model predicted, so that later the prediction can be checked against the microscope.

    CSV · JSON · 16-bit WAV per recording
    model prediction saved beside each recording, per window

C

The part that is actually research

not started Weeks of fieldwork with students and an entomologist. This is where the contribution is.

  1. 1  Record 55 identified mosquitoes, and 55 backgrounds

    One recording per individual insect, because two recordings of the same mosquito are not independent evidence — the same reason the training split was done by recording and not by window.

    for a sensitivity estimate to ±8 points, 95% CI: n ≈ 55
    to ±5 points: n ≈ 140
    for the Culex head: 55 confirmed Culex + 55 confirmed other species

  2. 2  Measure whether a model trained elsewhere transfers here

    Compare what the model said against what the microscope said. Nobody has this number for Indian mosquitoes. Either answer is a finding: if it transfers, that is useful; if it does not, that is more interesting, and it justifies every rupee of local collection afterwards.

    sensitivity · specificity · confidence intervals
    the app already stores every prediction beside its recording for exactly this

  3. 3  Publish the gap that nobody had measured

    Read from HumBugDB's own metadata rather than its headline totals. This is the strongest argument there is for funding Indian collection, and it is checkable by anyone in an afternoon.

  4. 4  Retrain on recordings from here

    The same script, the same architecture, the same verification suite, with local audio added. The export in Phase B exists to make this step possible.

    first model in existence to have heard an Indian mosquito

  5. 5  Turn it into surveillance

    Fixed sites, every week, through a monsoon. Relative abundance at one site tracked over time is a usable signal even when any single recording is uncertain — and it is the preliminary data a funded proposal needs.

    mosquito-minutes per night, per site, weekly
    users: district health office, NCVBDC programme

The honest limitation of the whole design

A sensor you must hold a phone inside of is not a surveillance network. At 5–10 cm the phone is a research instrument for building the dataset, not the deployed product. For coverage across a town the microphone has to live in the trap, which is hardware — and that is a different project.
